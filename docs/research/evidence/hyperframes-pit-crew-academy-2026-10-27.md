## the-brief

### 1. The brief

*Pit lane · 20 min · video 2:55 · stage pit-lane*

**Page:** https://hyperframes.studio/lessons/the-brief  
**Video:** https://static.heygen.ai/hyperframes-oss/pit-crew-academy/lessons/path-0.1-the-brief/explainer-r9.mp4  
**Captions:** https://static.heygen.ai/hyperframes-oss/pit-crew-academy/lessons/path-0.1-the-brief/explainer-r9.vtt

#### 1 · Watch — what the video says

**[0:00]** In this lesson you will write the first message that gets you a video you want to keep. It takes about a minute to write, and it saves you a lot of back and forth.

**[0:10]** Here is a first message that comes up all the time. Make a cool promo for my app. The agent has nothing to go on.

**[0:17]** It does not know the app, who will watch, how long it should run, or where it will play.

**[0:24]** So it asks, or it guesses. Answer with, just make it cool, and it guesses again. Back comes a landscape video with a stock phone, sized for nowhere in particular, and you are left wondering what to fix first.

**[0:38]** A brief fixes that before it starts. It is four things, and none of them take long to write.

**[0:45]** The goal: what the video is for, like an ad, an explainer or a launch post. Who it is for: the people you want to reach. Two to four specifics, like the length, the shape and what happens on screen.

**[0:59]** And the exact words you want on screen, in quotes, so the agent uses yours instead of inventing its own.

**[1:07]** Here is that brief, typed into the app. A twenty second vertical ad for a student budgeting app. Open on rent day, show three features, end on download free. A few short sentences, nothing more.

**[1:21]** More detail is not always better. A three hundred word spec, listing every font and every cut, is easy for the agent to misread and hard for you to check.

**[1:31]** Keep it to the few things you would be upset to see wrong.

**[1:35]** If you start from a website or a deck, the same rule applies. Give the link, then the brief. The agent reads the page for the facts, and your brief tells it what to make of them.

**[1:47]** If you start from the launch template, fill in every slot before you send it.

**[1:52]** Then add who it is for and where it will play. A template with empty slots gives the agent even less than a one line ask.

**[2:00]** If you have files, like a logo, a screenshot or a clip, attach them with Add instead of describing them.

**[2:07]** A file the agent can see beats a sentence about it.

**[2:11]** Often the agent replies with a plan or a question before it builds anything. This is the most useful moment in the whole session, and it is easy to miss.

**[2:22]** Do not leave it waiting.

**[2:24]** Answer it with specifics. Yes, but merge scenes four and five, and keep it under thirty seconds. Or, use real screenshots, I attached three. Every answer you give is one less thing it has to guess.

**[2:38]** So, before you send: say what it is for, who will watch, two to four specifics, and the exact words, in quotes. Attach what you have. And when the agent asks, answer. Next, you will turn a one line ask into a brief.

#### 2 · Recap — what the video said

1. A brief is the goal, who it is for, two to four specifics and the exact words in quotes.
2. **Keep it short** — the few things you would be upset to see wrong, not a full spec.
3. Attach your logo, screenshots or clips with Add instead of describing them.
4. When the agent shows a plan or asks a question, answer it with specifics.

#### 3 · Words to know

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `brief` | A goal, who it is for, and two to four specifics such as the length and the shape. |
| `quoted_words` | Words you want on screen, put in quotes so the agent copies them exactly. |
| `plan_reply` | Answering the agent's proposed plan with specifics, instead of staying quiet or saying ok. |

Each term has its own clip: `brief`, `quoted_words`, `plan_reply`.

#### 4 · Quiz

**Q1. You ask for "a launch video for my app" and get a generic one. Which ask would most improve the next try?**

- Make it better, more modern and more polished
- A goal, an audience and a few specifics ← **correct**
- The same ask again, with please added at the front
- A longer ask listing every font, color and cue

> A brief names the goal, the audience and a few specifics. That is enough to steer the agent without writing a page.

- *Why not* “Make it better, more modern and more polished”: A feeling gives the agent nothing to measure.
- *Why not* “The same ask again, with please added at the front”: Politeness changes nothing the agent can build.
- *Why not* “A longer ask listing every font, color and cue”: A long list is easy to misread; a short brief is not.

**Q2. You want the last card to say Try Orbit free. Why put those words in quotes?**

- Quotes make the words bigger on the card
- Quotes make the card stay up for longer
- Quotes make the agent copy them exactly ← **correct**
- Quotes pick which font the card will use

> Words in quotes are copied word for word. Words you only describe get reworded.

- *Why not* “Quotes make the words bigger on the card”: Size comes from what you ask for the card, not from quotes.
- *Why not* “Quotes make the card stay up for longer”: Length comes from a number of seconds.
- *Why not* “Quotes pick which font the card will use”: Fonts come from your brand kit or your words.

**Q3. The agent replies with a plan of six scenes and asks "Shall I go ahead?" What is the best reply?**

- Yes, but merge scenes four and five and keep it under 20 seconds ← **correct**
- Ok, go ahead and build it the way you planned it
- Whatever you think is best, you decide for me
- No, throw that away and start again from scratch

> Answering the plan with specifics steers it. Going quiet leaves every choice to the agent.

- *Why not* “Ok, go ahead and build it the way you planned it”: A bare ok hands every choice to the agent.
- *Why not* “Whatever you think is best, you decide for me”: That hands the decision back. Answer with the change you want.
- *Why not* “No, throw that away and start again from scratch”: Starting over throws away a plan that was mostly right.

#### 5 · Make it

**The prompt this lesson teaches**

> Make a 20 second vertical 1080x1920 ad for Orbit, a tool that turns one sentence into a launch film, for founders launching a product. Open on the problem, show three features, and end on a title card that says 'Try Orbit free'.

**Fix the one-liner** — Turn a generic 30 second landscape piece into a 20 second vertical ad for Orbit.

**Your own brief** — A short piece for your own product, site, deck or idea, from a brief you write.

### 2. Review and correct

*Pit lane · 20 min · video 2:29 · stage pit-lane*

**Page:** https://hyperframes.studio/lessons/review-and-correct  
**Video:** https://static.heygen.ai/hyperframes-oss/pit-crew-academy/lessons/path-0.2-review-and-correct/explainer-r9.mp4  
**Captions:** https://static.heygen.ai/hyperframes-oss/pit-crew-academy/lessons/path-0.2-review-and-correct/explainer-r9.vtt

#### 1 · Watch — what the video says

**[0:00]** Your first video is back.

**[0:02]** This lesson is about the next ten minutes, the part where a draft becomes something you would actually post.

**[0:09]** Start by watching it, start to finish, with the sound on. Make a note of what is wrong. Then send a message that says exactly what to change. That reply is what turns a generic draft into yours.

**[0:23]** The reply that does not work is, redo it, or make it better. The agent cannot see what you see.

**[0:30]** It rebuilds everything, and the parts you liked can change too.

**[0:35]** Relative words cause the same problem in a smaller way.

**[0:39]** Bigger, faster, a bit more punch. Bigger than what? You end up asking for the same fix two or three times.

**[0:48]** Instead, point at the thing.

**[0:49]** Choose Select, click the element in the preview, and it becomes a chip in your message. Now the agent knows exactly which title, which card, which scene you mean.

**[1:01]** Then say what it becomes.

**[1:02]** Not better, but the target.

**[1:04]** Use my homepage screenshot here, not the stock phone. Start this title at three seconds, and make it half the size.

**[1:13]** Some problems are not one element. Draw lets you circle a spot and add a note. Each note waits in Pending edits, so you can collect changes while you watch, and send them together.

**[1:24]** Several changes in one message are fine, as long as each one is exact. Three precise fixes in one reply land better than three rounds of vague ones.

**[1:36]** Some fixes are quicker by hand. Pick a clip on the timeline to trim it, move it or delete it.

**[1:42]** Drag an edge to shorten a shot. You do not need to ask the agent for a two second trim.

**[1:48]** Select an audio clip and its volume is right there in the timeline bar. If the music is too loud, pull it down yourself and listen again.

**[1:57]** And if anything goes wrong, by your hand or the agent's, Undo is one press away: Command Z. So try things. Nothing here is permanent.

**[2:08]** When it is right, press Export. Pick the quality, the format and the frame rate, then render.

**[2:16]** Then watch the exported file once, from start to end, before you send it to anyone. It is the version other people will see. Next, you will fix two things in a launch video and export it.

#### 2 · Recap — what the video said

1. Watch the first video all the way through, with sound, before you reply.
2. Point at the thing with Select or Draw, then say exactly what it becomes.
3. Several exact changes in one message are fine; vague ones like redo it are not.
4. Trim a clip or set a volume yourself on the timeline. Undo is Command Z.
5. Export, then watch the file once before you send it.

#### 3 · Words to know

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `pointer` | Click the thing in the preview, then say what changes. The agent knows exactly which element you mean. |
| `exact_change` | Say what changes and to what: a time, a size, the new words. Not "redo it". |
| `pending_edits` | Several changes waiting to be sent together as one request. |

Each term has its own clip: `pointer`, `exact_change`, `pending_edits`.

#### 4 · Quiz

**Q1. Your first video is back and the headline in scene 2 is wrong. What should you send?**

- Redo it, this is not what I wanted at all
- Point at the headline and say what it should say ← **correct**
- Make the whole video better and more polished
- Nothing yet, so it keeps all of its good parts while I think it over

> Point at the thing and say exactly what it should become.

- *Why not* “Redo it, this is not what I wanted at all”: That leaves the agent guessing what is wrong.
- *Why not* “Make the whole video better and more polished”: Everything at once, with no target, is the vaguest ask.
- *Why not* “Nothing yet, so it keeps all of its good parts while I think it over”: Going quiet is how most videos never get finished.

**Q2. Which correction will the agent follow exactly?**

- Make it better and a little more polished overall
- Redo it from the top, this is not what I meant
- Fix the problems you can see in the video, whichever ones matter most
- Start the headline at 3 seconds and make it smaller ← **correct**

> A time and a size are things the agent can do exactly. Words like better are guesses.

- *Why not* “Make it better and a little more polished overall”: "Better" has no target.
- *Why not* “Redo it from the top, this is not what I meant”: "Redo it" has no target.
- *Why not* “Fix the problems you can see in the video, whichever ones matter most”: It does not say which problems.

**Q3. After your correction, scene 2 shows both the new headline and the old one. What was missing?**

- It never said the new words replace the old ones ← **correct**
- A longer video so that the new words have room to fit in
- A different font for the whole scene to help it along
- Nothing: both headlines are meant to sit together in scene 2

> Say what the new words replace: the agent changes only what you name.

- *Why not* “A longer video so that the new words have room to fit in”: Length has nothing to do with it.
- *Why not* “A different font for the whole scene to help it along”: A font change does not replace words.
- *Why not* “Nothing: both headlines are meant to sit together in scene 2”: Both headlines were never meant to stay; the agent changes only what you name.

**Q4. You have three small changes to make. What is a good way to send them?**

- One at a time, waiting for each answer before the next
- Never together: the agent can only do a single change
- Together, as pending edits ← **correct**
- As one message that says make it all better please

> Each point, drawing or comment waits in Pending edits, so you can send several exact changes together.

- *Why not* “One at a time, waiting for each answer before the next”: Waiting is fine but slower; several exact changes in one message work too.
- *Why not* “Never together: the agent can only do a single change”: The agent can take several exact changes together.
- *Why not* “As one message that says make it all better please”: That throws away every target.

#### 5 · Make it

**The prompt this lesson teaches**

> In scene 2, change the headline to 'Meet Orbit Budget'. Keep scenes 1 and 3 as they are.

**Fix two things** — Fix two faults in a launch ad in at most three runs: a title over the app screen, and a call to action with the wrong words.

## logo-quick-win

### 3. A quick win: animate your logo

*Pit lane · 18 min · video 2:17 · stage pit-lane*

**Page:** https://hyperframes.studio/lessons/logo-quick-win  
**Video:** https://static.heygen.ai/hyperframes-oss/pit-crew-academy/lessons/path-1.1-logo-quick-win/explainer-r10.mp4  
**Captions:** https://static.heygen.ai/hyperframes-oss/pit-crew-academy/lessons/path-1.1-logo-quick-win/explainer-r10.vtt

#### 1 · Watch — what the video says

**[0:00]** A logo sting is a short animation of your logo, the kind that opens or closes a video. It is a great first project: small, clear, and you can finish one in a single sitting.

**[0:12]** Animate my logo, on its own, lets the agent decide everything. You might get ten seconds of particles and lens flare when you pictured a clean three second pop.

**[0:23]** A short brief fixes that.

**[0:25]** How long: five seconds. What shape: landscape for a video intro, or square for a post. Your logo file. And any words that should appear, in quotes.

**[0:36]** Attach the actual file with Add. An SVG, or a PNG with a transparent background, works best. If the agent has to redraw your logo from a description, it will not be your logo.

**[0:50]** Then say how it should move, in plain words. Grow in from the center. Slide up and settle. Draw the outline, then fill it. One move, said clearly, beats a list of effects.

**[1:04]** When the first version comes back, look at the motion.

**[1:08]** If it feels flat, ask for an overshoot, and say it as a number: grow to one point oh eight times its size, then settle back. That small bump is what makes a logo feel alive.

**[1:20]** More bouncy is the kind of ask that gets you three rounds of almost. A number gets you there in one, and if it is too much, you just change the number.

**[1:30]** If something else is off, point at it. Select the wordmark and say what changes: hold it on screen half a second longer, or fade it in after the icon lands.

**[1:41]** Say timing in seconds, not in feelings. Not quicker, but the whole sting in three seconds. Not later, but the tagline appears at two seconds.

**[1:52]** When it plays the way you pictured, export it. For an intro you will reuse, export one in each shape you need.

**[2:00]** So: a short brief with the length, the shape and your actual logo file. One move, in plain words. Then fix by pointing, with numbers and seconds. Next, you will tune a sting's overshoot, then make one with your own logo.

#### 2 · Recap — what the video said

1. A short brief is enough: how long, what shape, your logo file and any words in quotes.
2. Attach the logo itself, ideally an SVG or a transparent PNG.
3. Say one move in plain words, then tune it with numbers: overshoot to 1.08x, then settle.
4. Say timing in seconds, and fix one thing by pointing at it.

#### 3 · Words to know

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ease_out` | A move that starts fast and slows into place. |
| `ease_in` | A move that starts slow and speeds up as it leaves. |
| `overshoot` | Going slightly past the resting point, then settling back. |
| `linear_easing` | The same speed the whole way; it reads as mechanical. |
| `lockup` | The logo and the word together, moved as one unit. |

Each term has its own clip: `ease_out`, `ease_in`, `overshoot`, `linear_easing`, `lockup`.

#### 4 · Quiz

**Q1. In a 4 second logo sting, the logo shrinks and fades out at the end. Which ease should that exit use?**

- An ease-out, so it slows to a clean stop
- An ease-in, so it speeds up and leaves ← **correct**
- A linear ease, so it moves at one steady speed
- No ease at all, so it cuts out with no motion

> A leaving object starts slowly and speeds up, like something letting go.

- *Why not* “An ease-out, so it slows to a clean stop”: An exit does not stop. A move that slows down looks like it hits a wall on the way out.
- *Why not* “A linear ease, so it moves at one steady speed”: One steady speed is what reads as mechanical.
- *Why not* “No ease at all, so it cuts out with no motion”: A cut has no motion to ease, and it jars next to a soft enter.

**Q2. What is overshoot?**

- Moving at one steady speed all the way through the move
- Fading out before the move has finished playing
- Starting the move before the video itself starts
- Going past the resting point, then settling back ← **correct**

> The logo grows a little past its final size, then settles.

- *Why not* “Moving at one steady speed all the way through the move”: That is a linear move. It has no overshoot.
- *Why not* “Fading out before the move has finished playing”: That is a fade. Overshoot is about position or size, not opacity.
- *Why not* “Starting the move before the video itself starts”: That is timing. Overshoot happens inside the move.

**Q3. Your logo sting overshoots to 1.2x, which feels too bouncy. You ask the agent to make it less bouncy, and it comes back almost still, at 1.0x. What should the ask have said?**

- A target number, such as: overshoot to 1.08x ← **correct**
- Bounce a little more, but not too much overall
- Be smoother and more natural in how it moves
- Use a spring instead of an ease on the enter

> Less bouncy gave the agent no target, so it overcorrected to still. A number between the two, such as 1.08x, is a little less than 1.2x and still overshoots.

- *Why not* “Bounce a little more, but not too much overall”: Still relative, so the agent has to guess how much again.
- *Why not* “Be smoother and more natural in how it moves”: Smooth and natural name a feeling, not an amount.
- *Why not* “Use a spring instead of an ease on the enter”: Spring is a different tool. It does not say how much.

**Q4. A reviewer says your logo sting feels mechanical. Which fault is most likely?**

- The hold lasts too long on screen
- The logo is too large for the frame
- A move runs at one steady speed ← **correct**
- The enter is a little too slow

> Mechanical is the sound of linear easing: no start, no stop.

- *Why not* “The hold lasts too long on screen”: A long hold feels slow, not mechanical.
- *Why not* “The logo is too large for the frame”: Size changes how big it feels, not how it moves.
- *Why not* “The enter is a little too slow”: A slow enter feels calm. Mechanical comes from the speed curve.

#### 5 · Make it

**The prompt this lesson teaches**

> Make this logo sting 4 seconds. The logo lockup enters with an ease-out over 0.6 seconds with a slight overshoot, holds, then exits with an ease-in. No linear easing anywhere. Keep the logo, the word and the colors.

**Fix the sting** — Repair a sting that overshoots too far and leaves with the wrong ease, using a number in your ask.

**Your own sting** — A 4 second sting for your own logo, with the same enter and exit you have practiced.

#### 1 · Watch — what the video says

**[0:00]** Editing your own footage is where the agent saves you the most time. You give it a clip, and it does the cutting, the captions and the titles. This lesson is about getting that first cut close.

**[0:12]** Here is a typical starting point: a talking head clip on the timeline, anywhere from thirty seconds to a few minutes. You want a short, captioned version for a social feed.

**[0:24]** The quick ask is, make this a reel with captions.

**[0:27]** The agent has to guess how long, which part to keep, what the captions look like, and whether you want a title. It is easy to stop the run, unsure what is coming.

**[0:38]** The opposite does not work either. A long spec listing every cut, font and cue is easy to misread. And when two overlays come out wrong, it is hard to tell which line caused it.

**[0:51]** A footage brief is four things. The goal: the best forty five seconds. Where it plays: Reels, so vertical. Two or three edits, like word by word captions, white with a yellow highlight. And the exact words for the title card.

**[1:07]** Written out, it reads like this. One message, and the agent knows the length, the shape, the look of the captions, and the words on the title.

**[1:17]** Not sure which caption look you want? Open Captions in the side panel. Click a style and it goes into your message, so you can say, use this one, instead of describing it.

**[1:28]** When the first cut comes back, review it in an order that saves you rounds: overlays first, then the cut, then the sound.

**[1:36]** Overlays first, because titles and captions are the easiest to spot and the quickest to fix. Point at the title, and say when it starts and how big it is.

**[1:47]** Then the cut. If it dropped a line you need, say which line to keep and what to trim instead.

**[1:53]** For a cut, name the times, like from twelve seconds to eighteen. Pointing picks an element, not a stretch of time.

**[2:02]** For a small trim, do it yourself. Pick the clip on the timeline and drag its edge.

**[2:08]** Last, the sound. Listen with headphones. If music fights the voice, say where, and ask the music to make room for the voice there.

**[2:18]** So: a short brief with the goal, where it plays, two or three edits and the exact title words. Then review overlays, the cut and the sound, in that order, pointing and naming times. Next, you will fix a cut, then edit your own footage.

#### 2 · Recap — what the video said

1. A footage brief is the goal, where it plays, two or three edits and the exact title words.
2. **Not a long spec** — every extra line is one more thing to misread.
3. Pick a caption style from Captions instead of describing it.
4. **Review in order** — overlays, then the cut, then the sound.
5. For a cut, say the times; pointing picks an element, not a stretch.

#### 3 · Words to know

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `footage_brief` | The goal, where it plays, two or three edits and the exact title words. Not a long spec. |
| `captions` | The spoken words shown on screen, one or a few at a time. |

Each term has its own clip: `footage_brief`, `captions`, `pointer`.

#### 4 · Quiz

**Q1. You want your 30 second talking head cut for Reels. Which ask is most likely to get a video you keep?**

- Cut it to the best 20 seconds for Reels, add word by word captions, and open on a title that says 'Three mistakes' ← **correct**
- A 300 word spec that lists every cut, font and cue in order, with timecodes for each one
- Make this clip good and ready for social media, in a style that works for people scrolling
- Edit this clip so that it looks like it was made by a professional editor with taste

> A footage brief is the goal, where it plays, two or three edits and the exact title words.

- *Why not* “A 300 word spec that lists every cut, font and cue in order, with timecodes for each one”: A long spec is easy to misread; a short brief is not.
- *Why not* “Make this clip good and ready for social media, in a style that works for people scrolling”: No length, no edits and no words: it all gets guessed.
- *Why not* “Edit this clip so that it looks like it was made by a professional editor with taste”: It names no goal, no shape and no edits.

**Q2. The first cut is back. The title is in the wrong place and the music is too loud. What do you check first?**

- The music, then the captions, then the cuts
- The captions only, since they matter most
- The title and any other text on top of the video, then the sound ← **correct**
- Nothing: a first cut is always fine as it is

> Look at the title and other text on top of the video first, then the sound.

- *Why not* “The music, then the captions, then the cuts”: Text on top of the video is the easiest thing to spot in a footage edit, so look there first.

## glossary

### 7. Glossary

*Layering · 8 min · video 4:50 · stage layering*

**Page:** https://hyperframes.studio/lessons/glossary  
**Video:** https://static.heygen.ai/hyperframes-oss/pit-crew-academy/lessons/path-1.0-glossary/explainer-r2.mp4  
**Captions:** https://static.heygen.ai/hyperframes-oss/pit-crew-academy/lessons/path-1.0-glossary/explainer-r2.vtt

#### 1 · Watch — what the video says

**[0:00]** The agent knows the vocabulary of film and motion graphics.

**[0:04]** When you use its words, you get what you pictured.

**[0:07]** When you use vague ones, you get a guess. This lesson is the glossary: the words you will use all through this course.

**[0:15]** Start with the canvas. The canvas is the frame you build on: its length, its size, its frame rate and its shape. Say the canvas first, and everything after is built to fit it.

**[0:28]** A composition is one whole video project: its canvas, everything on it, and when. The timeline is the row that lays it out in time, left to right, with a playhead you drag to any moment. Everything you ask for sits at a time on the timeline.

**[0:45]** A frame is one still picture, and a video is frames played fast. F p s means frames per second: how many play each second.

**[0:54]** Film is twenty four, most video is thirty, and sixty is very smooth.

**[1:00]** These words let you be small and exact. A tiny gap between tiles is a guess. One frame apart, at twenty four f p s, is about four hundredths of a second, and the agent can set it.

**[1:14]** Resolution is the size of the picture in pixels, like nineteen twenty by ten eighty, called ten eighty p. Sizes in a message are in pixels, written p x, so one hundred twenty p x means the same thing every time. And the aspect ratio is the shape: sixteen by nine is wide, nine by sixteen is tall, for phones.

**[1:37]** Next, structure. A shot is one unbroken piece of picture with no cut inside it.

**[1:43]** A scene is a place and a moment, made of one or more shots. A storyboard is the whole shot drawn as still frames, one for each key moment.

**[1:54]** A beat is a change on screen that has a time, like a word appearing or a cut landing. And in this course, a layer is one message that adds one kind of thing to a shot. In an editor, layer can also mean one stacked element, so we say element for that.

**[2:12]** Music has beats too. B p m is beats per minute, the tempo. At one hundred twenty b p m a beat lasts half a second, so cuts on the beat land every half second.

**[2:25]** Now cuts. A hard cut is an instant switch. A dissolve fades from one scene into the next. A match cut carries a move across the cut. A zoom cut pushes in, cuts at the peak, and starts the next scene zoomed in. Name the one you want: if you only say cut, the agent may soften it into a dissolve.

**[2:46]** The camera can push in, moving closer so the subject grows, or pull out, moving away so it shrinks. A pan moves sideways instead, without getting closer. A slow push in on a headline gives it weight. A fast one gives it a jolt.

**[3:03]** Then motion. Every move is a tween: the frames between a start and an end, like scale from zero to one over four tenths of a second. Ease out starts fast and settles. Ease in starts slow and speeds up.

**[3:18]** Overshoot goes a little past the spot, then back. A stagger starts items one after another with a fixed gap.

**[3:26]** And a smear is one blurred frame that makes a fast move read as speed.

**[3:32]** Three more looks. Opacity is how see-through something is: a hundred percent is solid, zero is invisible, and a fade is opacity moving between them.

**[3:42]** A crop cuts away the edges so only part shows, which is how a panel can slide open without fading.

**[3:48]** And a loop plays again from the start, so ask for the end to match the start if it should repeat cleanly.

**[3:56]** And two words beginners forget.

**[3:58]** A hold is staying still on purpose after something lands, so the viewer can take it in; half a second after a headline is often what makes it readable. To scrub is to drag the playhead to a chosen time and look at the frame there. It is how you check a beat.

**[4:15]** Put them together and a message gets short and exact.

**[4:19]** A hard cut at two seconds. An ease out over four tenths of a second.

**[4:24]** Then a half second hold.

**[4:25]** That is a message the agent can build and you can check.

**[4:30]** So: the canvas, f p s and resolution, shots and beats, the four cuts, the camera, the motion words, the hold, and scrubbing. In the next step you see each one, answer a few questions, and use them in a short ask. Then you storyboard a real shot.

- The motion message moved a time that an earlier layer had set ← **correct**
- Tiles that overshoot always begin later than tiles that do not, by a few frames
- Naming an ease makes the agent delay the start of the move to match it
- An overshoot works by waiting a few frames before the pop begins at all

> Say that every earlier time stays, so a motion message cannot undo the timing.

- *Why not* “Tiles that overshoot always begin later than tiles that do not, by a few frames”: An overshoot happens inside the move. It does not move the start.
- *Why not* “Naming an ease makes the agent delay the start of the move to match it”: An ease shapes the move. It does not move when it starts.
- *Why not* “An overshoot works by waiting a few frames before the pop begins at all”: Nothing waits. The pop starts on its beat and passes full size on the way.

**Q4. The tiles bounce more than you wanted. What do you ask for?**

- Less bounce, please, and make it feel more subtle
- A gentler bounce that feels natural and fairly calm

- Fewer tiles so the bounce is less noticeable

> An overshoot as a number is something the agent can set, you can look at, and the Check can read.

- *Why not* “Less bounce, please, and make it feel more subtle”: Less and subtle are feelings, so the agent guesses a different amount each time.
- *Why not* “A gentler bounce that feels natural and fairly calm”: Gentler and calm give it nothing to set and nothing to check.
- *Why not* “Fewer tiles so the bounce is less noticeable”: Fewer tiles changes the piece, and the bounce is still there.

#### 4 · Quiz

**Q1. The timing layer comes back with the cut a full second late. What do you send next?**

- All four messages again from the storyboard, to be safe and start clean
- A new storyboard message, because the timing was built from the storyboard
- The timing layer again, saying the cut is at 3.04 s ← **correct**
- The motion message, since easing can pull a late cut back in

> Repair the layer that missed and name the miss.

- *Why not* “All four messages again from the storyboard, to be safe and start clean”: Layers one and three are already in the piece. Sending them again risks undoing them.
- *Why not* “A new storyboard message, because the timing was built from the storyboard”: The storyboard is fine. The miss is in the timing, so repair the timing.
- *Why not* “The motion message, since easing can pull a late cut back in”: Easing shapes a move. It cannot move a cut to its right time.

**Q2. The timing layer has just built. What tells you it is right before you send the camera and transitions layer?**

- A passing Check, which reads each layer as it lands in the piece
- The frames that layer named, looked at by their times ← **correct**
- Nothing yet: the whole shot is judged at the end, by watching it
- The agent saying the layer is finished and ready

> Look at the frames the layer names before you send the next. The Check comes once, after the motion and finish layer.

- *Why not* “A passing Check, which reads each layer as it lands in the piece”: The Check runs once, after the motion and finish layer. Early layers like timing are not the finished piece, so most checks would fail on purpose.
- *Why not* “Nothing yet: the whole shot is judged at the end, by watching it”: Waiting means a miss in the timing layer is already built on by the camera and motion layers.
- *Why not* “The agent saying the layer is finished and ready”: The agent saying it is done is not evidence. The frames are.
